# Printing and assembly guide

[中文](PRINTING.md) · English

`stl/` holds 54 parts, one STL each, at the default head-top height of 190 mm. The STL coordinates are the assembled positions, so you have to lay each part flat in your slicer; the orientations are below. The fit clearances between parts were set for FDM printing with a 0.4 mm nozzle and have not been tried on SLA.

> The orientations and settings on this page are suggestions computed from the model geometry. They have not been verified by actually printing. For a first print, try the small parts before the head shell to check the fits, then print the large ones.

## Preparation

**Materials and tools**

| Item | Spec | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Steel rod, aluminium rod or M3 screws | 3 mm | Hip pin 1 × 3×65 mm; knee pins 2 × 3×8 mm; ankle pins 2 × 3×12 mm |
| Brass rod | 2 mm, about 76 mm each, 2 in all | Antennae (the printed antenna is only 2.1 mm thick and breaks easily, so treat it as a spare) |
| CA glue or two-part epoxy | | All inlays, the small pieces inside the thigh, the feet |
| Primer, ivory spray paint, dark grey panel-line wash | | Painting (not needed if you just print in colours) |
| Small drill bits | 3.3 mm, 2.3 mm | Opening up holes that print too tight |

**Print settings (suggested)**

- 0.4 mm nozzle, PLA is fine. Use 0.12–0.16 mm layers for small parts and thin inlays. For clear threads on the rods and threaded pistons, use a 0.2 mm nozzle or 0.08 mm layers.
- Large parts such as the head shell: 2–3 walls, 10–15 % infill.
- The default height is 190 mm. For close to life size, export with `BD1_HEIGHT=316` (see the README). Pin holes and glue gaps keep their size, so the hardware does not change.
- Print one 1 mm inlay (for example `paint_right_panel`) first and try it in the pocket in the head shell. If the 0.2 mm glue gap per side is too tight or too loose, that is your printer's tolerance. Adjust the horizontal expansion setting, or change the gap in `bd1_model.py` and export again.

## Assembly steps

1. **Dry fit**: without glue, put all the inlays into their pockets and check they sit flat. File lightly where one is tight; never force it.
2. **Head**
   1. Glue the top band, the paint_* pieces on both sides and the right-eye ring into the head shell, following the pocket directions. The red and grey blocks on the hood must follow its curved face. The right (-y) and left (+y) patterns differ, so don't swap them.
   2. Glue the light panel to the floor of the recess at the back of the head.
   3. Each eye barrel goes into its 6 mm deep socket in the face panel, then the lens goes into the barrel. The right eye is the large steel-blue lens; the left is light grey.
   4. Ears: the grey centre piece goes into the side of the head by its own 3 mm peg, then the outer ring slides over it and is glued to the head.
   5. Antennae: slide on the antenna_collar first, then push the brass rod into the slanted 2.3 mm hole on the outer ear ring.
3. **Torso**: glue the chest plate to the floor of the chest pocket, and the red chest tab where the front meets the sloped top.
4. **Neck**: each end goes into an 8 mm slanted hole (one in the head shell, one in the torso).
5. **Thighs**
   1. First fit the dark-brown channel floor, the small tip piece and the dark-red block into the channel.
   2. Glue the rod to the channel floor with its thick end below the hex pocket, and glue the threaded piston into the side slot.
   3. Press the knee cap into the knee recess.
6. **Shins**: the red plate goes into a 1 mm pocket and stands proud of the shin, with a lug at each end.
7. **Feet**: butt-glue the dark rear half and the light front half at x = 40.5, with the ankle fork on the front half.
8. **Joints** (held by friction only; do not glue)
   - **Hip**: the hub on the inside of each thigh reaches into the round recess on the side of the torso, and one 3×65 mm pin passes through the left thigh, the torso and the right thigh.
   - **Knee**: one 3×8 mm pin per side, inserted from the inside of the shin, with the outside plugged by the short peg on the knee cap.
   - **Ankle**: one 3×12 mm pin per side, through the fork on the foot and the end of the shin.
9. **Stand (required)**: glue both feet into the 1.2 mm recesses in the stand. The centre of mass is about 4.5 mm behind the heel, so two feet alone cannot stand.

## Painting

Prime first, then spray ivory. The red, grey and dark parts are all separate pieces, so no masking is needed. Draw a red line in the 0.6 mm groove on the outer ear ring. Finally fill the panel lines, the ear ring groove, the thigh side slot and the through-slots on the feet with dark grey panel-line wash. Dirt, scratches, gradients and colour patches smaller than about 3 mm exist only in the reference texture, so weathering has to be painted by hand.

## Matching Bambu Lab PLA Basic colours

The values above were measured from the reference texture and are **not** matched to Bambu colour codes. The table below finds, for each value, the closest PLA Basic colour in Lab space. It is only a guide for choosing filament.

- The Bambu values come from [a list compiled by forum users](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/color-picker-include-hex-codes-and-closest-bambu-color-code/171046), not from official data. Trust the filament in hand and the official site for the real colour.
- Only colour names are given, not codes: most colours in that list have no code, and I have not verified them. A wrong code would send someone to buy the wrong filament.

## Troubleshooting

- **Holes too tight**: the pin holes are 3.3 mm and the antenna holes 2.3 mm. If your printer swells holes shut, run a drill bit through them.
- **Inlays won't sit flat**: usually first-layer edge spread (elephant's foot). Shrink the first layer a little, or chamfer the bottom edge before fitting.
- **Thin inlays warp**: the 1 mm red and grey inlays curl first. Use a cooler bed and a brim, or print several thin inlays together in the middle of the bed.
- **Does the head shell need support?** By the geometric estimate, the head shell (on its side) has about 2600 mm² of overhang. The README says it needs "almost no support". The two disagree and neither has been checked by printing. Trust your slicer's preview, and print one to see.
- **Loose joints**: if a friction pin hole is too loose, use a slightly thicker pin, or smear a very small amount of glue on the pin without letting it bond.
